If your Jaguar keeps needing coolant, or a temperature warning has brought a journey to a halt, the immediate concern is getting it assessed safely. The repair-or-sell decision comes after that.

A coolant warning is not proof that the head gasket has failed or the engine needs replacing. Equally, topping up repeatedly does not explain why coolant is being lost. This guide is for engine-cooling problems on petrol and diesel Jaguars; an electric I-Pace needs advice specific to its systems.

## Deal with the warning before the valuation

If the engine is overheating, stop somewhere safe and follow the instructions in the [handbook for your Jaguar](https://www.ownerinfo.jaguar.com/). Contact roadside assistance if you are unsure what to do or the car cannot safely continue. Do not drive it to a buyer to demonstrate the problem.

Never remove a coolant filler cap while the engine is hot. Jaguar's [published cooling-system guidance](https://topix.jaguar.jlrext.com/topix/service/archive/70720/maintenance.pdf) warns about scalding water and steam, and says persistent coolant loss needs qualified assistance. The same document contains model-specific instructions, so use your own handbook rather than borrowing its restart procedure.

The [RAC also warns](https://www.rac.co.uk/car-care/car-repairs/car-radiator-repair/jaguar) that driving with a coolant leak risks overheating and engine damage. There is no useful universal distance that makes a journey to a garage safe.

## Separate the original fault from any further damage

Two questions matter when the garage reports back: what caused the coolant loss or overheating, and has anything else been damaged?

Ask the technician to explain the evidence for each answer. If the quote is for a leak repair, ask whether it also addresses the overheating incident. If the recommendation is major engine work, ask what findings support that recommendation and what remains uncertain.

You do not need to choose the tests yourself. You do need to understand whether you are approving investigation, an initial repair or a complete solution. A warning message and an itemised diagnosis are different things.

## Give the garage a clear timeline

Write down when the issue began, which warning appeared and whether the car was recovered. Include any earlier coolant-related repairs and what happened afterwards.

If coolant has already been added, record when, how much and which product was used if known. Share those details rather than guessing. Do not run the engine again just to gather information, and do not conceal an overheating incident because the car subsequently started.

Keep the report and invoices. They will help if you seek a second opinion or decide to sell, and they reduce the need to explain the history from memory.

## Make the repair quote usable

Before approving work, ask for:

- The confirmed fault and any further investigation still required.
- An itemised total, including labour, parts, fluids and VAT.
- The checks that will be completed after the repair.
- The warranty on the work and any exclusions.
- A call before additional work exceeds your agreed budget.

If a substantial strip-down is proposed, ask what happens if it reveals more damage, including the cost of reassembly if you decide against further repair. A quote with that uncertainty clearly explained is more useful than a low opening figure that may not cover the final job.

## Is repair the right choice for you?

Repair may make sense if the fault is clearly identified and the rest of the car suits your needs. Look at the Jaguar as a whole, including other known work, rather than treating a coolant problem as an automatic reason to give up on it.
