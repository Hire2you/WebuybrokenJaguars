The garage has called. Your Jaguar has failed its MOT, and the repair quote is more than you wanted to spend. Now you have a decision to make: pay for the work, get another opinion, or sell the car with the faults still on it.

You can sell an MOT-failed Jaguar to us without repairing it first. We buy cars with no MOT and arrange free collection across mainland UK. But a failed test does not automatically mean selling is your best option. Start with what has actually failed and what it will cost to put right.

## Read the failure sheet before deciding

An MOT failure is a result, not a repair bill. Two Jaguars can both fail and need very different amounts of work.

The report separates defects into categories. Dangerous and major defects cause a failure; minor defects and advisories do not, by themselves. Ask the garage to explain which work is needed for a pass and which items are recommendations for later. [GOV.UK explains the MOT result categories](https://www.gov.uk/getting-an-mot/after-the-test).

Get a written quote that separates parts, labour and VAT. If the cause of a warning light or emissions problem still needs investigating, ask what has been confirmed and what remains an estimate. You want to know what you are authorising before the bill starts growing.

## Can you drive your Jaguar after an MOT failure?

Do not assume the old certificate means you can drive home. GOV.UK says you can take a failed vehicle away if its existing MOT is still valid and no dangerous defects were recorded. It must also remain roadworthy. If the report lists a dangerous defect, do not drive it until that defect has been repaired. [Check the official guidance on driving after a failure](https://www.gov.uk/getting-an-mot/after-the-test).

If the MOT has expired, ordinary driving and parking on the road are not permitted. Limited exceptions cover journeys to or from repairs and to a pre-arranged MOT test; the car must still be roadworthy. A trip to deliver it to a buyer is not one of those exceptions. [Read the rules for an expired MOT](https://www.gov.uk/getting-an-mot).

Selling to us avoids having to drive it anywhere. Tell us where the Jaguar is and we will arrange collection.

## Compare the repair bill with two realistic figures

If you are thinking of repairing the car just to sell it, you need three numbers:

- The likely selling price once the work is complete.
- The full cost of getting it ready for that sale, including repairs, diagnosis, retesting and any transport or selling fees.
- An offer for your Jaguar exactly as it stands, with collection costs accounted for.

Subtract the costs from the likely repaired selling price. Then compare what is left with the offer for the car as it is.

For illustration only, suppose a Jaguar might sell for £5,000 after repair. The work and other selling costs come to £1,800, leaving £3,200. If the offer for the car as it stands is £2,900 with collection included, repairing it creates a possible £300 extra return.

Those figures are an example, not a valuation or a repair estimate. They show why a higher selling price does not always leave much more in your pocket. You still have to fund the work, allow for uncertainty in the quote and find a buyer at the price you expect.

Use realistic comparisons for the same model, engine, age, mileage and condition. A dealer's advertised price is not necessarily what you will receive in a private sale.

## When repairing your Jaguar makes sense

If the failure is limited, the quote is clear and you want to keep the car, repairing it may be the sensible choice. A Jaguar you know and have maintained can be worth keeping, especially if replacing it would cost considerably more.

A second written quote from a Jaguar specialist can also help when the first estimate is high or the diagnosis is uncertain. Ask whether the proposed work addresses the cause of the problem and what the repair warranty covers.

Check the retest arrangements before moving the car. If it stays at the test centre for repair and is retested within 10 working days, the partial retest is free. Taking it elsewhere can change the fee, and other free or reduced-fee arrangements depend on the circumstances. [GOV.UK sets out the retest rules](https://www.gov.uk/getting-an-mot/retests).

## When selling it as it stands is worth considering

Selling becomes more attractive when the MOT work adds to problems you already knew about: a gearbox fault, an engine issue, repeated warning lights or damage you have been putting off repairing.

The point is to look at the whole car. Paying for the failed items may get it through the test, but it does not settle the cost of every other fault you are living with.

An MOT failure also does not make every Jaguar worth the same amount. We consider the model, engine, mileage, specification, history and condition when making an offer. No. We buy Jaguars without a current MOT, including cars that have just failed. Tell us the result and any other known problems when you request your offer.

Yes. Starting and being safe to drive are different things. We arrange collection for cars that cannot be driven, so you do not need to bring yours to us.

### Will a failed MOT mean I only get scrap value?

The failure alone does not tell you what the car is worth. The particular Jaguar, its condition and the work it needs all affect the offer. Compare actual quotes before choosing a route.
