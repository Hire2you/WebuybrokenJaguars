A gearbox warning can leave you expecting the worst, especially if your Jaguar has started changing gear harshly or will no longer move. Before agreeing to a replacement gearbox, find out what the diagnosis actually says.

We buy Jaguars with gearbox problems, including non-runners. You can request an offer with the fault still present, then decide whether repairing or selling makes more sense for you.

## A warning message is the starting point

Write down the exact dashboard message and describe what the car does. Does it hesitate when selecting drive, refuse reverse, lose drive after warming up, or stay in one gear? Say when the problem started and whether a garage has already inspected it.

Those observations help explain the situation. They do not confirm which component has failed. Avoid describing the car as needing a complete gearbox unless that has been established.

Jaguar provides [owner information matched to the vehicle and model year](https://www.ownerinfo.jaguar.com/). Use the instructions for your own car when a warning appears. If it loses drive, behaves unpredictably or cannot be moved safely, arrange qualified assistance rather than trying to drive it to a buyer.

## Questions to ask before accepting a repair quote

Ask for a written diagnosis and an itemised estimate. These questions make it easier to compare the work being offered:

- What tests support the diagnosis, and can you have a copy of the report?
- Is the quote for further investigation, a specific repair, a rebuild or a replacement unit?
- If a replacement is proposed, is it new, reconditioned or used?
- Does the price include labour, VAT, required fluids, any programming and checks after the work?
- What warranty is included, and what does it exclude?
- Could the price change after dismantling, and will the garage obtain approval first?

If you have a warranty or another repair agreement, check its terms and authorisation process before commissioning work. Do not assume the repair is covered or rule cover out without checking.

A quote for a used unit and a quote for a warranted rebuild may cover quite different work. Compare the scope as well as the total. A second opinion from a suitable transmission specialist can help if the first diagnosis is unclear.

## Is an oil change a repair?

Do not assume a transmission service will cure an existing fault. Ask the garage why it expects the proposed work to address your symptoms, and what happens if the problem remains.

For vehicles with a ZF transmission, [ZF supplies transmission-specific oils and service kits](https://aftermarket.zf.com/en/aftermarket-portal/our-portfolio/passenger-cars/products/oil-oil-change-kits/). That is a reason to identify the fitted transmission and follow the correct procedure, rather than apply a generic fluid or service interval to every Jaguar. It is not a promise that changing oil will restore a damaged gearbox.

## Compare keeping the car with selling it

If you want to keep your Jaguar, weigh up the repair, the rest of its condition and the cost of replacing it with a car that meets your needs. Money already spent is useful maintenance history, but it does not tell you whether the next repair is worthwhile.

If you would repair only to sell, compare the likely sale proceeds after all repair and selling costs with an offer for the car as it stands. Leave room for an uncertain diagnosis and a selling price that may differ from an advertised asking price.
