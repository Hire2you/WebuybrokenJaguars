A search for Jaguar XF turbo replacement costs can bring up a few hundred pounds for a part and a much larger garage quote. They may describe very different jobs. Before deciding whether to repair the car, you need the exact engine, a confirmed diagnosis and a price for the complete work.

If the garage has only reported low boost or restricted performance, ask what testing establishes that the turbo itself has failed. If replacement is confirmed, use the checks below to compare the quote with an offer for the XF as it stands.

## How much does an XF turbo replacement cost?

There is no single fitted price that covers every XF. Engine specification, which turbo is affected, access for removal and any related damage change the job considerably.

For a concrete **parts-only example**, Turboworks advertised a [remanufactured 49477-01200 turbo for selected XF 2.2 diesel applications](https://turboworks.co.uk/shop-online-turbochargers-actuators-chra-gasket-kits-turbo-parts-sell-buy-turboworks/752-remanufactured-turbo-turbocharger-49477-01200-land-rover-jaguar-22-d-td4-sd4-150-200hp.html) at £399 including VAT when checked on 13 October 2026. Its exchange terms list a £70 surcharge unless the old unit is sent first, refundable subject to the stated return conditions.

That is a supplier listing, not a fitted repair quote or a recommendation to order that part. It does not cover the garage's diagnosis, removal and fitting, or any additional work your car needs. Compatibility must be checked against the original unit and vehicle details; prices and terms can change.

Even “2.0 diesel XF” is not enough detail. Jaguar introduced a [240PS twin-turbo Ingenium diesel for the 2018 model year](https://media.jlr.com/jaguar/news/2017/02/jaguar-f-pace-xf-and-xe-deliver-more-efficiency-more-performance-and-more-convenience). Do not assume every engine of the same capacity has the same turbo arrangement or replacement cost.

## What confirms a turbo fault?

Loss of power, smoke, unusual noise and oil consumption warrant investigation, but none identifies a failed turbo on its own. [BorgWarner's diagnostic guidance](https://www.borgwarner.com/aftermarket/boosting-technologies/troubleshooting) lists other possible causes, including leaking air pipework, restricted airflow, exhaust restrictions and engine or lubrication faults.

Ask for the fault codes and the test findings in plain English. What was inspected? Was a leak found? Is the problem with the turbo, its control system or something elsewhere? A scan result should be the start of that explanation, not the whole explanation.

If all you have is a dashboard message, our [restricted-performance guide](/blog/jaguar-restricted-performance-warning) covers what to record before diagnosis. Where a diesel exhaust-filter problem has been identified, read the [Jaguar DPF guide](/blog/jaguar-dpf-warning-repair-or-sell) as well.

## Stop testing it if the symptoms are getting worse

Do not repeatedly accelerate to reproduce smoke or prove that boost has disappeared. [Garrett's system-check guidance](https://www.garrettmotion.com/turbo-replacement/turbo-diagnostics/turbocharger-system-diagnostics-guide/) says trials should stop if signs such as black smoke, excessive oil consumption or uncontrolled engine revving appear.

If serious symptoms develop on the road, stop somewhere safe and arrange professional assistance. Do not restart a damaged engine to demonstrate it to a buyer. Follow the warning instructions for your exact Jaguar and tell the recovery operator what happened.

## Check what the garage is actually quoting for

Ask for an itemised written estimate covering:

- **The replacement unit:** part number, new or remanufactured, and any old-unit exchange charge.
- **The cause of failure:** what has been found and what work addresses it.
- **Associated work:** relevant oil-feed and return checks, pipework, fluids, filters and cleaning or replacement of affected components.
- **The fitted total:** diagnosis, labour, VAT and checks after installation.
- **The warranty:** what parts and labour are covered, and any conditions attached.

Not every car needs every item replaced. The garage should explain which work is necessary on yours. BorgWarner warns that a turbo failure can result from another engine problem, so replacing the turbo alone may leave the original cause unresolved.

If further dismantling is needed to establish the damage, agree an investigation limit and ask to be contacted before extra work starts. Check any existing warranty approval requirements before commissioning repairs.

## Repairing to keep it, or repairing just to sell?

If you like the XF and the rest of it is sound, a defined repair with clear warranty terms may make sense. Consider other known maintenance and how long it will be unavailable, as well as the immediate invoice.

Repairing solely to sell requires a different calculation. Compare the realistic sale proceeds after the full repair and selling costs with a current offer for the car unrepaired. Money already spent on diagnosis should not force you into another payment that does not suit your plans.
